Half your applicants never finish the screen. It is not a random half.

myCareer is a candidate-side career record with verified provenance. It gives you evidence about the people your funnel is currently losing, and it does it without adding a scoring tool to your stack.

−50%

A 2026 randomised field experiment across interview formats found one-way asynchronous screens caused an over 50% drop in application continuation, steepest among women and the most qualified candidates. The same body of research finds AI screening performs well on those who complete it: roughly 12% more offers and 18% more starts. The instrument is not the problem. The abandonment is.

Everyone you lose there is someone you already paid to acquire

They applied. They were sourced, marketed to, or referred. Then they hit a webcam and a fixed question list, and the ones with options walked. You carry the acquisition cost and get nothing, and the loss is concentrated in exactly the segment your hiring managers complain they cannot find.

The deep dive

When a candidate believes a filter has them wrong, they pay a few dollars once for a guided interrogation of exactly the requirements their record failed to evidence. They keep the record and reuse it for every future application.

  • Scoped by the gap report, not a generic question list
  • Produces an evidence packet a human reads
  • Charged at record creation, never per application
  • No outcome is promised, ever

What you receive is evidence, not a score

01

Did it, scoped it, or enabled it

Every claim records whether the person did the work, scoped it and handed it off, or enabled a team to do it. On a résumé those are the same sentence.

02

Numbers with a baseline and a source

Each figure carries what it measures, what it was before, and whether the candidate can still produce the source. Anything unsourceable is omitted rather than softened.

03

Equivalence, so you stop missing people

A requirement for ISO 27001 surfaces the candidate whose record says SOC 2, and says why: the two share twelve evidence clusters, and one programme produces the evidence for both. Keyword search drops that person silently.

04

An explicit list of what is not evidenced

The report names the gaps and what to ask about them. A tool that only reports matches is a tool that flatters.

It does not add a compliance obligation to your stack

This is deliberate and it is enforced in the code. NYC Local Law 144 attaches to automated employment decision tools that screen or rank candidates: annual independent bias audit, published results, ten days' notice, and the obligation sits with the employer using the tool. The EU AI Act treats employment AI as high risk with obligations from December 2027.

myCareer produces evidence for a person to read. It emits no fit percentage and sorts no candidate list, so it is not the regulated thing, and it happens to supply what those regimes ask for anyway: explanation, human oversight, and a route for a candidate to contest an outcome. If you ask us for a score, the answer is no.

Three ways to work together, smallest first

No build

Accept the packet

Tell candidates a myCareer record may be attached to an application, and read the ones that arrive. Costs nothing, needs no integration, and tests whether the evidence is worth your recruiters' time.

Light

Offer it at the rejection

Candidates your filter declines get told what was not evidenced and can appeal with evidence. Self-selection keeps the volume low: a forty minute interrogation is a costly signal, so only people who genuinely believe you have them wrong will do it.

Integrated

In your ATS or careers site

Requirements in, evidence packet out, alongside the application. Postings are already read from schema.org job markup, so the pipe is short. This is the stage that needs a partner, which is why we are talking.

What we are asking for

A pilot on one requisition family, ideally somewhere with compliance or regulatory weight, where you already suspect the filter is losing people. We want to learn two things: whether recruiters find the packet worth reading, and whether the candidates it surfaces are ones you would otherwise have missed.

Nothing is asked of your engineering team for the first two rungs. Candidate data stays with the candidate: they generate what is shared and can read exactly what it says. Transcripts are not retained.
