# What we can charge, and for what

Researched 18 September 2026. Sources at the bottom. Design guidance, not legal advice.

---

## 1. You are right about willingness to pay

Reverse recruiting in 2026 charges **$1,500 to $10,000+ per month**, frequently with a $500 to
$2,000 initiation fee on top, and sometimes a success fee of around 10% of first-year salary.
People pay it.

Against that, three dollars is not a price objection. It is a rounding error. Anyone who argues
that job seekers will not pay a small fee for a career tool has not looked at what job seekers
are already paying.

So: **the price is not the barrier.** Agreed, fully.

## 2. The barrier is classification, and it is a bright line

Charging a job seeker money puts you near employment agency licensing, which most US states
regulate and some enforce hard. California draws the clearest line, and it is unusually
favourable:

> Under Cal. Civ. Code § 1812.501, a person who engages **solely in the preparation of résumés
> and cover letters** is exempt from the advance-fee job-listing rules, provided they **do not
> advertise or hold themselves out as offering other job seeking or placement services**, and
> charge **no more than $300** to any single customer.

Two conditions. The second one, the money, we clear by two orders of magnitude. The first one is
where the product design decides the answer.

California also prohibits placement agencies from charging job seekers for finding employment,
and from charging applicants any fee before a job is secured. Other states vary; several require
a licence, bonding, or fee caps to charge a job seeker anything at all.

## 3. The rule that follows: sell the record, never the application

This is the whole conclusion, and it costs nothing to comply with:

| Framing | What it is | Classification risk |
|---|---|---|
| "Pay $3 to appeal this rejection" | a fee tied to one employer's decision | reads as a job-seeking or placement service |
| "Build your record. $3, once, yours forever. Attach it to anything, including this." | a document preparation tool | résumé preparation, the exempt lane |

Same money, same button, same user action. Entirely different category.

**The test to apply to every page of copy:** could someone buy this and use it with no job
application in sight? If yes, you are selling a tool. If the purchase only makes sense in the
context of a specific employer's decision, you are selling access to that decision, and that is
the regulated thing.

Three consequences for the build:

1. **The record must be purchasable and useful with no role attached.** It already is. Someone
   with no live application can run the interrogation, get their documents, and never apply
   anywhere. Keep that path first-class and visible, not a fallback.
2. **The appeal is a use of the artifact, not the product.** "Attach your record to this
   application" is a feature of a thing you already own. It is not what the three dollars bought.
3. **Charge at record creation, never at submission.** The moment a fee is collected per
   application, the framing collapses no matter what the marketing says.

## 4. Never guarantee anything

The exposure in reverse recruiting is not the price. It is the guarantee.

Published examples in the market include an unqualified headline promise of "nine interviews in
your first three months or your money back," qualified elsewhere by an undefined "for most
clients," with "interview" quietly redefined to include recruiter calls. Industry comparisons
note that many guarantees are free extensions rather than cash refunds.

That pattern is deceptive advertising, and it is the reason the category will eventually attract
enforcement. Reported job-scam losses to the FTC went from $90 million to $501 million between
2020 and 2024, and attention follows money.

**Our position is the opposite on all three axes**, and it should be stated plainly on the
pricing page because it is a competitive advantage, not a disclaimer:

- **Small fee**, not a large one.
- **No outcome promise.** Not interviews, not offers, not callbacks. We do not control hiring
  and will not pretend to.
- **The asset is delivered regardless.** You get a verified record whatever any employer does.
  There is nothing to refund because you already have the thing you bought.

A product that promises nothing about outcomes cannot fail to deliver on outcomes.

## 5. Do not price relative to reverse recruiting

The temptation is "$3 instead of $3,000." Resist it.

Positioning against them puts you in their category, in candidates' heads and in search results.
Worse, that industry runs on distress: people months into unemployment paying for hope. Demand
driven by desperation is not a market to build on, and undercutting it still means competing for
the same desperate customer.

Price and describe this against **what it is**: a tool that produces a career record you own.
The comparison that flatters is a $300 résumé writer who hands you a document, or the $2,000 to
$5,000 executive résumé service that started this whole project. Not the $10,000 a month
guarantee merchants.

## 6. The recommended shape

- **Free path, always.** Runs on the candidate's own Claude or ChatGPT subscription, or
  on-device. Never remove this. It is what makes "nobody pays to be considered" literally true.
- **A few dollars, once, for a hosted run.** Buys convenience: no setup, no API key, no
  uploading a skill. Not access, not consideration, not an outcome.
- **Charged once, not per application.** The record is durable. The twentieth application costs
  nothing, and that fact belongs on the pricing page in large type.
- **Employer-sponsored codes.** An employer who wants signal on candidates their filter was
  about to lose can cover the fee. Costs them far less than the acquisition they already paid
  for, and it removes the fee question entirely for that candidate.

## 7. Before any money moves

- Get counsel on employment agency licensing in the states you will operate in. The rules vary,
  the California exemption is not universal, and a 50-state review exists precisely because this
  is a patchwork.
- Have counsel read the pricing page, not just the terms. Classification turns on how the offer
  is described, and the marketing copy is the offer.
- Keep the refund policy simple and generous. At three dollars, refunding on request costs
  almost nothing and removes the only complaint that could ever escalate.
- Say what the fee buys in one sentence, on the page, in plain words: *a verified career record
  you own and can reuse, produced by an interview that takes about forty minutes.* No outcome
  appears in that sentence, and none should.
